System	A group of elements and their relations. Maintains existence through mutual interaction of parts. <strong>More than the sum of its parts.</strong>	systems
Open System	Must <strong>interact with the environment</strong> to maintain its existence. Examples: people, organisations.	systems
Closed System	Does <strong>NOT</strong> need to interact with the environment. Examples: atoms, molecules.	systems
Emergence	Behavior of the <strong>whole cannot be fully predicted</strong> from the behavior of individual parts alone.	systems
Self-Organization	The ability to <strong>organize structure or behavior without external control.</strong>	systems
Nonlinearity (Complex System)	Small changes in one part can have a <strong>significant, disproportionate effect</strong> on the system as a whole.	systems
Adaptation	The system can <strong>change its behavior or structure</strong> in response to changing environmental conditions.	systems
Hard Systems Thinking	<strong>Optimizes design</strong> using defined performance criteria. Uses quantitative techniques. Ignores social/personal dynamics.	systems
Soft Systems Thinking	<strong>Improves conceptual model</strong> via SSM. No final/optimal answer. Learning process never stops. Stays connected to personal aspect.	systems
Normative Model	Describes what <strong>SHOULD</strong> be done. Evaluation based on criteria.	systems
Descriptive Model	Describes what is <strong>ACTUALLY</strong> being done. No judgment.	systems
Deterministic Model	All variables are <strong>known and precisely determined</strong>. No randomness.	systems
Stochastic Model	Accounts for <strong>randomness and uncertainty</strong>.	systems
Well-Structured Problem	We know the procedures, have all necessary information, and know how to obtain it. Example: operational control.	systems
Time (in models)	Represented as a <strong>continuous variable</strong> or as <strong>discrete intervals</strong>.	systems
Probability	Quantifies the <strong>likelihood of an event</strong> occurring. Range: <code>0</code> (impossible) to <code>1</code> (certain).	systems
Who developed SSM?	<strong>Peter Checkland</strong>, late 1960s, University of Lancaster, UK.	ssm
SSM Stage 1	<strong>The Problem Situation (Real World)</strong> — Acknowledge and explore the problem area in an unstructured way.	ssm
SSM Stage 2 — Rich Picture	Cartoon-like diagram depicting the situation. Must include: Structures, Processes, Climate, People, Issues, <strong>Conflicts</strong>.	ssm
SSM Stage 3 — Root Definition	Structure: <em>"A system to do <strong>X</strong>, by <strong>Y</strong>, in order to do <strong>Z</strong>."</em>	ssm
CATWOE	<strong>C</strong>ustomers, <strong>A</strong>ctors, <strong>T</strong>ransformation, <strong>W</strong>eltanschauung, <strong>O</strong>wners, <strong>E</strong>nvironmental constraints.	ssm
Weltanschauung (W in CATWOE)	The underlying <strong>belief system/worldview</strong> that makes the Transformation meaningful.	ssm
SSM Stage 4 — Conceptual Model	Logical diagram of <strong>7±2 activities</strong> (verbs in imperative) necessary to carry out the Transformation.	ssm
SSM Stage 5	<strong>Comparison</strong> — use a matrix to compare each activity with the real world. Does it exist? How is it measured? Is it good?	ssm
SSM Stage 6	<strong>Desirable and Feasible Changes</strong> — Owner Analysis, Social System Analysis, Political Analysis.	ssm
SSM Stage 7	<strong>Action to Improve (Real World)</strong> — Implement changes; may trigger a new cycle of SSM inquiry.	ssm
Holon (SSM)	A conceptual model (system description) in SSM that does NOT represent the real world directly — used to structure thinking.	ssm
Objective Function	A <strong>linear expression</strong> to be maximized (profit) or minimized (cost).	lp
Feasible Region	The set of all points satisfying <strong>all constraints simultaneously</strong>.	lp
Optimal Solution location	Always at one of the <strong>corner points (vertices)</strong> of the feasible region.	lp
Slack Variable	Represents <strong>unused resources</strong> in a ≤ constraint. If slack > 0, constraint is non-binding.	lp
Surplus Variable	Represents <strong>excess over minimum</strong> in a ≥ constraint.	lp
Shadow Price	Rate of change in the objective function per <strong>1-unit increase in RHS</strong> of a constraint.<br/><code>New Profit = Old Profit + SP × ΔRHS</code>	lp
Shadow Price of non-binding constraint	Always <strong>ZERO</strong>. Non-binding means slack > 0 (resource not fully used).	lp
Sensitivity Analysis	Studies how changes in <strong>model parameters</strong> (obj. coefficients or RHS values) affect the optimal solution.	lp
Allowable Increase/Decrease (obj. coeff.)	The range over which the <strong>optimal corner point remains the same</strong>. Quantities unchanged; profit changes by <code>ΔPrice × Quantity</code>.	lp
Excel Solver — LHS Rule	LHS cells MUST contain active <code>=SUMPRODUCT()</code> formulas. <strong>Never hardcode LHS values!</strong>	lp
Simplex Method	Algebraic algorithm that moves from <strong>corner point to corner point</strong> along the feasible region boundary to find optimum.	lp

Excel Solver Profit Cell formula	Formula: <code>=SUMPRODUCT(decision_variables, objective_coefficients)</code>.	lp
Who developed I-O Analysis?	<strong>Wassily Leontief</strong> — Nobel Prize in Economics 1973.	io
Technical Coefficient aᵢⱼ	Value of inputs from sector i needed to produce <strong>1 unit of output in sector j</strong>.<br/><code>aᵢⱼ = zᵢⱼ / Xⱼ</code>	io
Leontief Matrix	The matrix <code>(I - A)</code>, where I = identity matrix, A = technical coefficient matrix.	io
Leontief Inverse (I-A)⁻¹	Also called <strong>Multiplier Matrix M</strong>. Represents <strong>direct + indirect inputs</strong> needed to satisfy 1 unit of final demand.	io
I-O Core Equation	<code>x = Ax + y</code> → <code>x = (I-A)⁻¹ × y</code><br/>Total output = Leontief inverse × final demand vector.	io
Intermediate Consumption	Goods and services purchased by industries from <strong>each other</strong> as inputs for further production (matrix Z).	io
Final Demand (y)	Production sold to <strong>final consumers</strong>: households, government, export, investment.	io
Value Added (Primary Inputs)	Production costs NOT purchased from other sectors: wages, depreciation, EBIT, taxes, imports.	io
I-O Row Identity	<code>Xᵢ = Σzᵢⱼ + yᵢ</code> — Total output of sector i = sum of intermediate deliveries + final demand.	io
Price Index formula	<code>Ip = (I - Aᵀ)⁻¹ × X̂⁻¹ × Sum Vector</code><br/>Sum Vector = W·Iw + M·Im + E·Ie	io

Markov Property (Memorylessness)	Next state depends <strong>ONLY on the current state</strong>, NOT on the sequence of past states.	markov
Transition Matrix P — key rule	The <strong>sum of each row must equal exactly 1</strong>.	markov
State Vector at step t	<code>π⁽ᵗ⁾ = π⁽⁰⁾ × Pᵗ</code>	markov
Trajectory Probability	<code>P(s₀,s₁,s₂) = P(X₀=s₀) × P[s₀→s₁] × P[s₁→s₂]</code>	markov
Absorbing State	Once entered, <strong>cannot be left</strong>. <code>P[i][i] = 1</code>.	markov
Transient State	Once left, there is a <strong>positive probability of never returning</strong> to it.	markov
Communicating Class	A set of states where every state can reach every other state. Symbol: <code>i ↔ j</code>.	markov
Closed Class	A communicating class that <strong>cannot be left once entered</strong>.	markov
Irreducible Markov Chain	The entire state space is <strong>one single communicating class</strong>. Can go from any state to any other state.	markov
Absorbing Markov Chain	A chain where every state can <strong>eventually reach an absorbing state</strong>.	markov
Stationary Distribution π	Solve <code>πP = π</code> AND <code>Σπᵢ = 1</code>. Long-run equilibrium distribution.	markov
Hitting Probability hᵢₐ	Probability of <strong>ever reaching set A</strong> starting from state i.	markov
Expected Hitting Time mᵢₐ	Expected <strong>number of steps</strong> to reach set A starting from state i.	markov

Binomial Distribution formula	<code>P(X=k) = C(n,k) × pᵏ × (1-p)^(n-k)</code><br/>where <code>C(n,k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!)</code>	stoch
Poisson Process	Events occur at constant average rate λ. Arrivals in fixed time → <strong>Poisson distribution</strong>. Time between arrivals → <strong>Exponential distribution</strong>.	stoch
M/M/1 Notation	<strong>First M</strong>: Markovian arrivals (Poisson). <strong>Second M</strong>: Markovian service (exponential). <strong>1</strong>: one server.	stoch
Utilization ρ (M/M/1)	<code>ρ = λ/μ</code>. Must be <strong>&lt; 1</strong> for a stable queue.	stoch
P₀ — probability of no waiting	<code>P₀ = 1 - ρ</code>	stoch
Lq — average queue length	<code>Lq = ρ² / (1 - ρ)</code>	stoch
L — avg customers in system	<code>L = λ / (μ - λ)</code>	stoch
W — avg time in system	<code>W = 1 / (μ - λ)</code>	stoch
Wq — avg time in queue	<code>Wq = ρ / (μ - λ)</code>	stoch
Kendall notation M/M/s	<strong>M</strong> = Markovian/exponential, <strong>s</strong> = number of parallel servers.	stoch

Positive Link (+/S)	Variables move in <strong>SAME direction</strong>. A↑ → B↑. A↓ → B↓.	cld
Negative Link (−/O)	Variables move in <strong>OPPOSITE directions</strong>. A↑ → B↓. A↓ → B↑.	cld
Reinforcing Loop (R)	<strong>EVEN number</strong> of negative (O) links. Amplifies change → exponential growth or decay.	cld
Balancing Loop (B)	<strong>ODD number</strong> of negative (O) links. Seeks equilibrium, opposes change, goal-seeking.	cld
Delay in CLD	Time lag between cause and effect. Indicated by a <strong>double line (‖)</strong> crossing the arrow.	cld
Limits to Growth archetype	Reinforcing loop drives growth → hits constraint → Balancing loop kicks in and slows growth. <strong>Action: Push before constraints hit.</strong>	cld
Tragedy of the Commons	Individual self-interest in a shared resource leads to overexploitation. Two reinforcing individual loops + two balancing commons loops.	cld
Shifting the Burden	Quick fix relieves symptoms (B1) but creates dependency that weakens the long-term solution (B2). Reinforcing side-effect loop R3.	cld
Escalation archetype	Two competitors each escalate in response to perceived threats. Two Balancing loops together form a Reinforcing escalation spiral.	cld
Fixes that Fail	Fix relieves symptom (B1) but triggers delayed Unintended Consequence that worsens the problem (R2).	cld
Growth and Underinvestment	Growth drives demand → strains performance → investment avoided → performance standard lowered. Three loops: R1, B2, B3(delays).	cld
Drifting Goals (Eroding Goals)	When performance fails, the <strong>goal itself is lowered</strong> instead of fixing performance. Two Balancing loops: correction (B1) and goal erosion (B2).	cld
Accidental Adversaries	Partners\' local optimization actions unintentionally undercut each other\'s success after a delay. Undercutting links (U) with delay markers.	cld

Computer Simulation	Imitation of a real-world system over time. Used when analytical models are <strong>mathematically impossible</strong> or too complex.	sim
Discrete Event Simulation (DES)	Variables change state only at <strong>discrete points in time</strong> (events). Example: bank customer arrivals.	sim
Continuous Simulation	States change <strong>continuously over time</strong>. Example: fluid in a pipeline, System Dynamics models.	sim
Monte Carlo Simulation	Uses <strong>repeated random sampling</strong> from probability distributions to model uncertainty and calculate output distributions.	sim
Agent-Based Simulation (ABS)	Models individual <strong>autonomous decision-making agents</strong> that interact, producing emergent system-level behavior.	sim
System Dynamics (SD)	Approach to understand <strong>nonlinear behavior of complex systems</strong> over time using stocks, flows, feedback loops, table functions, and delays.	sim
