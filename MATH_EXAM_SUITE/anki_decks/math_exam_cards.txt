Characteristic equation for y'' + py' + qy = 0	r² + pr + q = 0<br><br>Solve for roots r₁, r₂:<br>• 2 distinct real → C₁e^(r₁x) + C₂e^(r₂x)<br>• Repeated root  → (C₁+C₂x)e^(rx)<br>• Complex α±βi  → e^(αx)[C₁cos(βx)+C₂sin(βx)]	odes
Two distinct real roots r₁, r₂	y = C₁e^(r₁x) + C₂e^(r₂x)<br><br>C₁, C₂ determined by initial conditions.<br>The two exponentials are linearly independent.	odes
Repeated root r (multiplicity 2)	y = (C₁ + C₂x)·e^(rx)<br><br>The extra factor of x ensures linear independence<br>when we only have one root.	odes
Complex roots r = α ± βi	y = e^(αx)[C₁·cos(βx) + C₂·sin(βx)]<br><br>Euler's formula: e^(iβx) = cos(βx) + i·sin(βx).<br>Real solution uses cos & sin enveloped by e^(αx).	odes
Variation of Parameters formula (y_p)	y_p = y₁·∫(−y₂·g / W) dx  +  y₂·∫(y₁·g / W) dx<br><br>where W = Wronskian of y₁, y₂<br>g(x) = right-hand side of non-hom. ODE	odes
Wronskian W(y₁, y₂)	W = y₁y₂' − y₂y₁'<br><br>If W ≠ 0 on interval → y₁, y₂ are<br>linearly independent → valid fundamental set.	odes
Non-homogeneous ODE — general solution	y = y_h + y_p<br><br>y_h = homogeneous (complementary) solution<br>y_p = particular solution<br><br>Superposition principle.	odes
Partial derivative ∂f/∂x	Differentiate f(x,y,...) with respect to x,<br>TREATING all other variables (y, z, ...) as constants.<br><br>Example: f=x²y³ → ∂f/∂x = 2xy³	multivariable
Gradient ∇f  (2 variables)	∇f = (∂f/∂x,  ∂f/∂y)<br><br>Points in direction of steepest ascent.<br>|∇f| = magnitude of steepest slope.<br>∇f ⊥ level curves.	multivariable

Hessian determinant D	D = f_xx·f_yy − (f_xy)²<br><br>D > 0, f_xx > 0  →  Local MINIMUM<br>D > 0, f_xx < 0  →  Local MAXIMUM<br>D < 0             →  SADDLE POINT<br>D = 0             →  Inconclusive	multivariable
Chain rule for z=f(x,y), x=x(t), y=y(t)	dz/dt = (∂z/∂x)(dx/dt) + (∂z/∂y)(dy/dt)<br><br>Sum of partial derivatives times rates of change.<br>Chain rule extends naturally to more variables.	multivariable
Clairaut's Theorem (mixed partials)	If f_xy and f_yx are continuous at (a,b), then:<br><br>f_xy(a,b) = f_yx(a,b)<br><br>Order of differentiation doesn't matter.	multivariable

Directional derivative formula	D_u f = ∇f · u<br><br>where u is a unit vector (|u|=1).<br><br>Measures rate of change of f in direction u.<br>Maximum rate = |∇f| in direction of ∇f.	multivariable
